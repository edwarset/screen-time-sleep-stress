Limitations & responsible interpretation

Population mismatch

The full dataset spans ages 18–59 and eight occupations. Only 1,861 records have occupation “Student”; college enrollment is not recorded. Conclusions about college students must therefore be qualified.

Observational data

The dataset does not establish temporal order or control for all possible confounders. Strong correlations are not proof that screen use causes poor sleep. Data provenance and sampling details should be checked before generalizing.

Unavailable measures

Actual bedtime, gaming-specific exposure, streaming-specific exposure and GPA are absent. Those research subquestions cannot be answered with this dataset.

Next steps

Investigate student-only associations, control for stress and other covariates, and collect college-specific survey data with validated sleep-quality measures for stronger conclusions.

Results-section summary

Across 15,000 observations, daily screen time was negatively correlated with sleep quality (r = −0.747), but not meaningfully correlated with sleep duration (r = −0.002). Pre-sleep phone use (r = −0.335) and stress (r = −0.857) were also negatively associated with sleep quality. The student-labeled subgroup (n = 1,861) showed a similar screen-quality association (r = −0.743). These findings are descriptive and correlational; they cannot establish causality or be generalized confidently to all college students.
